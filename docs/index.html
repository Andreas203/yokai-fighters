The pitch

Bind a yokai. Keep its power. Pay the debt.

Each run crosses a province of eight rows, one fight per row. Win a duel and the yokai is sealed into a paper talisman. You draft one of three abilities from its power, and your fighter takes shape from the spirits you chose to hunt. Chase the Kitsune and you finish a zoner. Chase the Kappa and you finish a grappler.

The run ends at the Tanuki, a cheerful trickster who copies the special you have invested in most. You can see it coming all run. The question is whether you can out-think your own best move.

Background

A province folded into an illusion

It is the end of autumn in rural Japan, and the Tanuki has folded the whole province into an illusion. The yokai inside it are real spirits, trapped and made to fight again each time the loop closes. When Ryo falls, he wakes at the province’s edge and walks in again.

The tone is mischievous folklore with a melancholy edge, never horror. Yokai are tricksters and lonely spirits, not monsters. A yokai that wins does not hurt Ryo. It leaves, tails swaying, between the lanterns. The mood is dusk, paper screens and things about to end.

Design pillars

Six promises the game has to keep

01

Every upgrade changes how you fight

Piercing Foxfire cuts through the Kitsune’s own projectiles. It changes the matchup, not just a damage number.

02

Your fighter is yours

Watch one fight and you can tell which yokai the player hunted.

03

Every choice costs something

A replaced move loses its levels. A dojo makes you choose between healing and training.

04

Easy to start, deep to master

Kihon is a simple scheme for newcomers. Kata rewards precision for fighting-game fans.

05

The game answers your habits

The Nine-Tailed Kitsune reflects any special you use twice in a row.

06

Readable, fair fights

Yokai react only to what is on screen. A loss should trace back to a decision you made.
