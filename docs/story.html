Story outline

A loop Ryo has to walk out of

A trickster has folded a province into an illusion, and an apprentice exorcist keeps walking back in. This is the story as it is planned, told in short cards.

The setup

The illusion loop

The Tanuki has folded a rural Japanese province, at the end of autumn, into an illusion. Every run is one pass through it. The yokai inside are real spirits, trapped and made to fight again each time the loop closes.

When Ryo falls, the loop closes and he wakes at the province’s edge to walk in again. Each time the Tanuki beats him, up to three times, the yokai react faster and lean aggressive. In the story, the illusion is growing stronger.

The rule

Binding borrows. A debt must be named.

Ryo’s master, Sōen, taught him one rule: binding a spirit borrows its strength, so the debt must be named. Ryo takes this seriously. At every binding the round-ending blow slows, an ink stroke seals the yokai into a paper talisman, and he apologises to it.

After a loss his talismans are blank again. The exception is a single talisman that stays faintly inked if the run reached row 6 or further. That special starts the next run at Level 2.

“Forgive me, {yokai}. I’ll set your spirit free.”Ryo · shown at every binding

The twist

The merchant is the Tanuki

A cheerful travelling merchant waits at the row 4 rest stop, and sometimes again at row 7. He heals, sells and gives advice. He is the Tanuki in disguise, helping Ryo grow so that there is more to steal.

At row 4 he says which of Ryo’s moves he would love to have, and it is exactly the move the Tanuki will copy. Players who listen can see the boss coming. At the Tanuki’s node, in every run that gets that far, the disguise drops.

“That {move} of yours... I’d love one.”The merchant · row 4

The Tanuki’s boss theme borrows the merchant’s cheerful melody and slowly detunes it.

Story outline

How the story is told

The story arrives as short cards of one or two sentences, so it never holds up the run for long. This is the full schedule. No other beats are planned.

  1. First run · before row 1

    Master Sōen’s rule: name the debt.

    Intro card · Master Sōen
  2. Every binding

    “Forgive me, {yokai}. I’ll set your spirit free.”

    Ryo
  3. Row 4 · merchant, still disguised

    “That {move} of yours... I’d love one.”

    The merchant
  4. First loss

    The Tanuki’s cheerful voice names the move it would have stolen. The player learns the copy rule before ever meeting the boss.

    Wake-up card · narrator, with the Tanuki’s voice
  5. Later losses

    Wake-ups vary by the row reached and the yokai that won. The yokai that won never gloats. It leaves, or goes back to what it was doing.

    Narrator
  6. Every run that reaches the boss

    The merchant drops his disguise at the Tanuki’s node.

    The reveal · narrator
  7. First win

    Why the Tanuki collects techniques.

    Narrator and the Tanuki
  8. Second win

    The Tanuki points out that Ryo’s talismans are cages too.

    The Tanuki
  9. Third win · the ending

    Ryo binds the Tanuki and releases every yokai, his own included: “I said I’d set you free.”

    Ryo

“You keep them in paper, I keep them in my sleeves,” the Tanuki laughs. “Which of us is the collector, little exorcist?” Ryo has no answer yet.

LOSS · IN THE GAME NOW

{yokai} slips off between the lanterns, tails swaying, and does not look back. Somewhere a bound thing is still waiting for someone to return it.

The tone checks every card passes: someone is teasing or bargaining, something is owed or left behind, and nothing is gory, cruel or frightening.
